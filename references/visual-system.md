# Visual system

Read this file when choosing composition density, materials, or decorations.

## Density presets

### Airy

- Best for one photo or a quiet cover.
- Photo area: 45–60%.
- Decorations: 4–6, mostly flat.
- Dimensional props: 0–1.
- Breathing space: 25–35%.

### Balanced / medium density

- Default for all outputs unless the user requests another density.
- Photo area: 55–65%.
- Single-photo decorations: 6–8, including labels and hardware.
- Multi-photo decorations: 8–10, including labels and hardware.
- Dimensional props: 1–2.
- Breathing space: 12–25%.
- One obvious hero, 2–3 medium frames, remaining sources as clear details.

### Rich editorial

- Use only when the user explicitly wants a dense scrapbook.
- Photo area must remain at least 48%.
- Decorations: 12–16, mostly paper, ink, thread, or shallow objects.
- Dimensional props: at most 3.
- Preserve at least one calm zone for title or background.

Density is not the number of objects alone. Richness can come from paper edges, writing, overlaps, stitching, translucent layers, and crop rhythm without adding more 3D props.

## Composition families

### Elevated collage island

Place the assembled cluster slightly above center with open background around it. Good for covers and for scenes with sky, water, or landscape.

### Notebook field

Use one open or spiral notebook as the base plane. Let photographs interrupt the writing area rather than filling every page edge. Good for diary and friendship stories.

### Memory wall

Build around a card, corkboard, postcard, or file-folder shape. Use a route line or thread sparingly to connect selected moments, not every photo.

### Editorial stack

Layer a small number of large photo frames with paper excerpts and a strong title. Good when one portrait or scene should dominate.

Avoid equal grids unless the user explicitly asks for a contact sheet.

## Locked-photo fallback frames

When source fidelity is uncertain, use clean rectangular photo windows that can receive the untouched originals after background generation. Keep tape, clips, paper edges, lettering, and decorations outside the window bounds. Do not use torn photo masks, perspective-warped frames, curled photo corners, irregular cutouts, or foreground objects crossing the image area in this mode.

The frame itself may have a white or colored mat and a shallow shadow. Preserve breathing room around it so deterministic placement does not cover meaningful decorative work. Richness should come from the surrounding paper construction rather than from altering the source photograph.

## Decoration derivation matrix

Choose from the row matching the sources. Do not use every item.

| Source cue | Possible motifs |
| --- | --- |
| Road, transit, city | folded map, route line, directional arrow, parking stub, road-number label, thin metal clip |
| Mountain, trail, forest | contour drawing, pressed leaf, seed envelope, field-note tag, stitched fabric patch, bark rubbing |
| Sea, lake, rain | ripple vellum, wave stitching, shell specimen label, blue glassine, weather note, small real shell |
| Friends, family, portrait | caption strip, date tab, fabric swatch, handwritten quote, tiny doodle, photo-corner tabs |
| Food, cafe, meal | menu fragment, napkin fold, ingredient sketch, chopstick sleeve, shallow plate silhouette, receipt strip |
| Art, books, calligraphy | rice paper, ink stroke, type specimen, library slip, book ribbon, small red ink mark |
| Nightlife, music | ticket stub, waveform, colored foil, lyric fragment, stage-light acetate |
| Flowers, fruit, season | pressed petal, produce label, seed-paper fragment, color swatch, hand-cut silhouette |
| Found object, souvenir | specimen label, catalog number, tiny envelope, tracing, location note |

Then add neutral accents from a separate pool: torn notebook strip, vellum tab, binder clip, thread, paperclip, staple, corner label, sparse stars, dots, or underlines.

## Anti-repetition rule

For each output, form a project-specific set:

- `content motifs`: 3–5 selections from source cues;
- `neutral accents`: 2–4 selections;
- `hardware/natural object`: 0–2 selections;
- `forbidden repeats`: any prop that appeared in two recent outputs or was rejected by the user.

Change shape and construction as well as object identity. Two cameras with different colors are still the same repeated idea. Two identical circular seals with different letters are also repetition.

## Material contrast

Use meaningful contrasts:

- sharp glossy photo against fibrous paper;
- translucent vellum over opaque notebook stock;
- soft fabric beside thin metal hardware;
- torn handmade edge beside a clean printed label;
- subtle real shadow under a dimensional object and almost no shadow under ink.

Avoid applying the same grain, blur, outline, and drop shadow to every layer.

## Tape construction

Good tape shows at least three of these cues:

- uneven torn fiber edge;
- slightly darker overlap where tape crosses paper;
- a ridge or fold that changes highlight direction;
- one lifted or curled corner;
- local wrinkles rather than evenly spaced stripes;
- a soft contact shadow on one side;
- mild translucency revealing the material below.

Vary tape materials: masking tape, translucent stationery tape, cloth tape, paper washi, or narrow book-repair tape. Match color to the palette instead of aging every piece beige.

## Text hierarchy

- Title: expressive, 2–7 words.
- Supporting sentence: one short sentence, ideally under 18 words.
- Labels: 1–3 words each.
- Optional journal copy: use only when enough clean paper is visible.

Generated lettering can be irregular and human. Critical names, dates, prices, addresses, or long copy should be rendered after generation when exactness matters.
