# Packaged visual anchors

These bundled images are the Skill's visual calibration set. They are not optional marketing examples: inspect the relevant files before planning or rendering so a clean conversation receives the same composition baseline as an experienced one.

## Reference roles

- **User photos are content references.** They alone define people, pets, places, objects, food, signs, and factual visual content.
- **The packaged examples are style-only references.** They define composition, material depth, photo-to-background integration, lettering scale, decorative density, and the relationship between the outer stage and scrapbook island.
- Never copy a person, place, caption, date, logo, or factual object from a packaged anchor into the user's work.
- If the renderer supports role-separated references, label the user photo `CONTENT — preserve exactly` and the packaged example `STYLE ONLY — copy visual grammar, not content`.
- If the renderer cannot accept a style image, inspect the anchor and transcribe its visual fingerprint into the render prompt. Do not skip calibration merely because only one reference image is accepted.

## Primary anchor — all single pages and summaries

Always inspect `examples/after/08-open-sky-stage.png` once per run and use it as the primary style-only reference for every page. It is the user-approved open-background reference, bundled unchanged with the Skill so a new installation does not need the development conversation or an extra attachment. It is not a test output or an additional source photo.

Visual fingerprint:

- a large photographic sky with delicate cloud variation, cool blue distance, a warm sunset band and low dark treetop silhouettes creates a real sense of air and depth;
- the outer scene is continuous behind the bounded paper-and-photo island, with open space above and along its sides, not a paper texture colored blue;
- expressive light English lettering can occupy the sky above the island without requiring a large opaque title panel;
- layered warm papers, shallow hardware shadows, slanted card edges and selective perimeter contacts embed the photographs in one connected assembly;
- the summary uses a tall card and staggered wide/support cards joined by common backing and thread, leaving the scenic stage legible rather than filling it with scraps.

For a **single page**, borrow the outer sky/horizon construction and material integration, then design a smaller one-photo story island with its own title, note and playful objects. Do not copy the anchor's four-photo layout. For a **summary**, retain the scenic stage and connected, unequal card hierarchy while adapting to the actual source count and aspect ratios.

Borrow the scenic grammar, not the exact sky pixels, caption, people, lake or photographs in the anchor. The outer background is decorative; it must not be presented as the user's actual location or used to extend/repaint a source. Do not transfer the anchor's portrait canvas ratio: final outputs remain exact 3:4.

## Supporting material anchors

These older examples may help with paper, tape, object and photo-edge construction. Inspect one only if those details need extra calibration. Their flat or page-filling backgrounds, photo scale and sparse object mixes are **not** the current default; the primary open-sky anchor and SKILL.md take precedence. If reference slots are limited, retain all original sources and the primary anchor first, and omit these supporting examples.

### Quiet portrait, cafe, home, or lifestyle page

Inspect `examples/after/04-a-quiet-cup.webp`.

Visual fingerprint:

- a clearly visible pale outer stage surrounds a compact story island;
- the photo sits high in the composition and is large enough to remain emotionally dominant without filling the page;
- grid paper, irregular knit fabric, torn notes, a clip, foliage, and one small ceramic object share shallow contact shadows;
- the title and journal copy occupy a meaningful lower zone rather than a tiny footer;
- paper, cloth, metal, photograph, and ceramic remain visibly different materials;
- neighboring materials touch or overlap the photo card so it feels assembled into the page, not pasted into a blank rectangle.

### Sea, road, landscape, or travel page

Inspect `examples/after/06-coastal-air.webp`.

Visual fingerprint:

- a large upper photo, coastal map strip, blue striped fabric, translucent wave paper, rope, tag, and shell form one connected island;
- the photo edge is crossed by tape/material at selected perimeter points, while faces and important content remain clear;
- the title is expressive and large; supporting copy, tide chart, map, and specimen tag create narrative density;
- the outer stage remains visible and calm while the island carries the complexity;
- materials are irregular and asymmetrical, not aligned as a square template.

### Food, evening, museum, or object-led page

Inspect `examples/after/01-dinner-after-blue-hour.webp`.

Visual fingerprint:

- the photo occupies roughly half the composition and is integrated with a menu strip, folded fabric, tape, title paper, illustrated food, and a numbered tag;
- dark outer color provides atmosphere outside the lighter scrapbook island;
- foreground objects partly cross supporting papers rather than floating independently;
- the result has a strong vertical rhythm and rich material contrast without turning into a full-frame pile of props.

### Multi-photo material connections

Inspect `examples/after/07-life-lately-summary.webp`.

Visual fingerprint:

- every source appears once in an asymmetric hierarchy, with one clear hero, two medium supports, and smaller secondary cards;
- frames differ in size, aspect, and anchor position; shared papers and labels connect them into one island;
- no equal 2×2 grid, four disconnected rectangles, or central vertical title column;
- the title sits outside the photo cluster and the outer stage remains visible;
- photo edges, backing papers, tape, perforated notes, clips, and material overlaps form a single physical assembly.

## Calibration sequence

1. Inspect the primary open-sky anchor before writing the set plan, for single and combined pages alike. Supporting material anchors are optional.
2. Name three anchor traits that will be carried into the new page: one spatial trait, one material/integration trait, and one density or lettering trait.
3. Adapt those traits to the user's actual source aspect ratio and subjects; do not trace the anchor layout mechanically.
4. Render the entire physical scene together whenever the image tool can edit with references. Never generate a decorative background containing a blank photo opening and then treat deterministic insertion as the preferred workflow.
5. Compare the result with both the original source and the anchor fingerprint. Reject it if the photo looks like a hard rectangle placed over a prebuilt background, if edges show halos or jaggies, if a single page becomes a centered-photo template, or if a summary becomes an equal grid. Also reject a flat blue/beige paper or cloth background, a token strip of scenery, or a mostly dark outer field; the atmospheric sky and distant light must be visible on every page unless the user requested another background.

The anchors provide a stable visual language, not a fixed template. Vary palette, objects, title, frame construction, and layout according to the user's photos while preserving the same level of integration and storytelling.
